§32j — smudge panels (hover, focus or tap) · §32e — footnote

Three facts about forgetting

Headlines are free. The detail is smudged chalk — it clears where your attention lands, and nothing on the board moves. The pattern was first measured by Ebbinghaus1Footnote 1Hermann Ebbinghaus tested his own memory of nonsense syllables in the 1880s and plotted how fast they faded..

Forgetting is fast at first

Most of what fades, fades in the first day or two. After that the curve flattens: what survives the first week tends to stay.

Effortful recall is the workout

Re-reading feels productive but asks nothing of memory. Pulling the answer out — even slowly — is what strengthens the path.

Each review buys more time

After a successful recall, the next fade is slower. That is why the gaps between reviews can grow: one day, three, a week, a month.

§32f — duo panes (lean into a side)

Same hours, two plans

Six hours of study, spent two ways. Hover or focus a pane — it takes the room, and its depth opens without moving the page.

Cramming one long night

6 h × 1

Feels strong on the night. Most of it is gone within a week.

Massed study builds fluency — the material feels familiar because you just saw it. Familiarity is not retrieval strength, so the test a week later finds the path already overgrown.

Spacing six short sessions

1 h × 6

Feels harder each time. Much more of it is still there in a month.

Each session starts with a little forgetting, so every recall takes effort — and effortful recall is the workout. The difficulty you feel is the learning, not a sign it is failing.
